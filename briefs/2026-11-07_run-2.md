## Done

### 1. A new insights section: sequencing traps (insights.html#sequencing)

The timeline page already lists every dated fact for the cycle. What it does
not say out loud is that for an applicant carrying a portfolio or a research
supplement, the application deadline is frequently not the binding date. The
new section re-sorts dates already on the site by which one arrives first, in
three groups, twelve items, 22 citation markers, every source `official`:

- A portfolio's deadline is not the application's deadline. Stanford moves the
  Common App itself forward for arts-portfolio applicants (15 Oct for a 1 Nov
  REA round; 5 Dec for a 5 Jan RD round) — the only school of the 30 that does.
  Brown's SlideRoom portfolios (4 Nov) and Princeton's arts and Cornell's music
  supplements (6 Nov) fall *after* the 1 Nov application date. MIT's portfolios
  share the application date. Three different patterns in one cycle.
- Research material travels a different channel at each school: inside
  SlideRoom with a mentor letter (MIT), through the school's own portal only
  after submission (Johns Hopkins, Swarthmore), inside the Common App's
  Additional Information with the full paper explicitly unwanted (Harvey Mudd),
  or not at all (Duke).
- Test dates and system windows are hard edges: MIT EA and Caltech REA require
  the test to have been *taken* by 30 Nov; UC's filing window is 1 Oct–30 Nov
  with no ED or EA; Swarthmore's portal does not open until mid-October.

One item is tagged `[推論]`: that the date to plan against is the earliest of
the application, the portfolio upload, and a mentor's letter the applicant does
not control. The rest are `[事實]`.

### 2. Source importer: 40 cited sources were missing from the site

`scripts/import-sources.js` only parsed sources written as a markdown table.
Two notes list theirs as YAML flow maps under the front-matter `sources:` key
(`- {id: V01, url: "...", ...}`), so both silently contributed zero sources:
the browser-verification note (V01–V19) and the blocked-pages note (21 leads).
Their citations therefore could not be used anywhere on the site.

The importer now parses that form too, and keeps an id that already carries its
note's code rather than doubling it, so the browser-verification note's own
internal references (`(V02)`) match the site's citation keys exactly.

- sources: 1387 → 1427. Keys lost: 0 (checked key-by-key against the previous
  generated file, because a renaming bug here would silently break citations
  across every page).
- Both groups now render on sources.html: "Browser verification of
  excerpt-only items (19)" and "Leads only (pages not read) (21)".
- The new insights section uses one of them (V02) for Columbia.

### 3. The three unreadable hosts, retried and then written up as a worksheet

`admission.princeton.edu`, `admissions.northwestern.edu` and
`admissions.purdue.edu` were retried with two independent request paths and
four URLs per host. All twelve failed: "navigation denied" in the browser,
"certificate rejected" on the fetch path, no response body, no CAPTCHA or
human-verification page, no bypass attempted. Two paths failing identically on
three hosts makes this a property of this machine's network path, not of one
tool, so a later unattended run should not be expected to clear it.

Rather than leave Q002 as a standing wish, it now lists those twelve URLs with
the exact question each one answers, so the pages can be read by hand once and
pasted back in a few minutes. The retry is recorded at the end of
research/2026-11-07_browser-verification_v1.md, and two decisions were added to
DECISIONS.md: these hosts stay "not stated" until pasted in, and essay prompts
stay paraphrased rather than reproduced verbatim.
