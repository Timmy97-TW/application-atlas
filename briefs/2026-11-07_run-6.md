# Work this run did

## 1. The excluded names were in a committed file, and are not any more

The names check exists so that certain words never reach a committed file in a
public repo. Run 5's brief recorded that the check had passed **by quoting the
pattern it runs**, which put those words into a committed file in the public
repo — the precise thing the check prevents.

- `main` was never affected. The site was never affected. The leak existed only
  on the public branch `run/2026-11-07-integrate`.
- The brief line now reports that the check ran and found nothing, without
  spelling the pattern out.
- CLAUDE.md rule 7 now says a brief reports the check and never writes the
  pattern, and why.

**Not fixed, and it needs Timmy's decision:** the redaction corrects the working
tree and every state from here on, but the old text is still reachable in that
branch's **git history** (commit `a93fbf0`). Removing it from history means a
force-push, which rule 10 forbids and which is the maintainer's call, not a
scheduled run's. The words are three short tokens in one line of one brief, in a
branch that will be squashed or merged shortly; the practical options are to
leave it, or to rewrite that branch's history before merging.

## 2. The link checker was blaming universities for its own failures

Run 5's link check reported 21 connection errors, 20 of them
`ERR_HTTP2_INVALID_SESSION` against two Stanford hosts and `hmc.edu` — pages
earlier runs had read without trouble. Re-requesting all three over HTTP/1.1
this morning returned HTTP 200 from every one.

The failure was Node's `fetch` negotiating HTTP/2, not the universities. This is
the **third** time a fetching path's own failure has been written up in this repo
as a property of a university's server; the first two were Princeton,
Northwestern and Purdue, which the 06:15 run then read without difficulty.

`scripts/check-links.js` now retries any connection-level failure once over
HTTP/1.1 before reporting it, records which status came from the retry, and
prints a separate labelled list of URLs that failed over HTTP/2 and answered
over HTTP/1.1. 403 and 404 are untouched — those are answers, not failures to
connect.

### The link check now reads honestly

944 unique URLs, **54 not OK** (run 5: 74). Nothing deleted.

- **45 × HTTP 403** — the familiar client-level refusals: `williams.edu`,
  `engin.umich.edu`, `admission.virginia.edu`, `ox.ac.uk`, `ucl.ac.uk` and the
  rest. A 403 to a script is not a school failing to publish; both Williams and
  Michigan open in a browser, and the site says so in each case.
- **8 × HTTP 404** — six already-logged dead pages (Amherst × 2, AIT, UIUC
  archive, Olin CDS, UCAT key dates) plus two LEAD-level guessed URLs on
  Princeton and Northwestern that nothing is cited from.
- **17 rescued** — every one of the Stanford and Harvey Mudd URLs above now
  returns 200 over HTTP/1.1 and is reported as our client, not the host.
- **1 connection error** — `dev.admissions.cornell.edu`, the non-public
  development subdomain already on the record with its claim withdrawn.

No citation on the site rests on a URL in this list that was not already
flagged.

## Purdue's letters policy — closed `[事實]`

Purdue Undergraduate Admissions states it in its own voice:

> "Letters of recommendation aren't required, but if a student submits one,
> we'll include it in their review."

Three things the site now says together, because they look contradictory and are

stated: how many letters, from whom, by what route.

## Princeton's "not looking for perfect" — closed, and it is a blog post `[事實]`

It exists. The admission office's own blog, bylined **Dean of Admission Karen
Richardson '93**, dated **1 September 2026**:

> "We are not looking for perfect. We're looking for authentic."

and in its closing paragraph, "Remember that we are not looking for perfection
but rather for you to be perfectly YOU."

A search of **all 1,248 URLs** in `admission.princeton.edu/sitemap.xml` found the

## Michigan and Williams major-level admission — closed `[事實]`

This was E9's remaining hole: run 5 read both schools' central admissions pages
but not the college and major pages.

**Michigan does not admit to the university.** "At Michigan, you don't apply to
the university – you apply to one of our 15 undergraduate schools and colleges."
Ten units take first-year applications and Engineering is one of them, so
engineering is a **direct admit at the point of application**; biology has no
major-level admission and goes through LSA, which tells applicants to "apply
specifically to the College of Literature, Science, and the Arts".

- Every applicant writes a college-specific essay, 100–550 words. Engineering
  sets no essay of its own and is **absent** from the published list of units
  with additional requirements; the one exception is Integrated Business and
  Engineering, which "must also complete the Ross Admissions Portfolio".
- Engineering's criteria "are based on the strength of your academic preparation
  to pursue an engineering degree", emphasising precalculus, chemistry and
  physics, and "No special class rank, grade point average, test score, or other
  qualification by itself will assure admission."
- Moving in later is expensive: a year in the home unit first, C-or-better
  prerequisites, a 55-credit ceiling, and GPA 2.5 "is not guaranteed admission".

That combination — the decision made at application time, and an unusually
costly way back in — is the planning point, and it is on the site as `[推論]`.

**Williams names no major at application** (declared "spring of sophomore year"),
and the first-year checklist carries no major, school or programme field. This is
recorded as an **absence on the pages read**, not as an affirmative statement by
Williams, and the inference is tagged. Williams awards only the B.A. and its
catalog majors list has no engineering — but it is not a flat no: the 3-2 with
Columbia and the 2-1-1-1 with Dartmouth's Thayer School each run five years, and
**the engineering degree is conferred by the partner, not by Williams**. Whether
either programme can be elected at application is not stated.

## Northwestern's ISP — closes halfway, and the other half is Q007

Published, and now on the site: the separate second application; the
admitted-to-Northwestern-first order; **either round** ("You may apply to both
ISP and Northwestern under either early or regular decision", so ISP does not
force ED); the ISP deadline falling **after** Northwestern's in both rounds,
the reverse of ordinary supplement sequencing; late applications still read but
"will put you at a disadvantage due to the limited number of spaces"; four years
of mathematics including calculus and four of science including chemistry and
physics; and AP/IB scores having to arrive or ISP "may delay making a decision".

**What the ISP application asks is stated nowhere**, and the trail loops: the
requirements page, under its own "Supplement materials:" heading, says only "For
an updated list of required materials, please see the ISP website", and that page
enumerates nothing — no prompts, no word limits, no form, nothing about letters.
The 2026–27 ISP deadlines are not published either: the live table is last
cycle's, every date on it has passed, and the ISP page calls the deadline closed.

A fillable ISP form does sit on the official ISP domain and is the only document
anywhere with real prompts. **This repo deliberately cites nothing from it**: its
metadata gives a 2015 creation date, it requires SAT Subject Tests (discontinued
2021) and asks for a fax number, no current page links to it, and its stated
March 1 deadline contradicts the published table. Citing it would put an
eleven-year-old form on the site as this cycle's requirements. It is written up
in the note so a later run does not rediscover it and mistake it for current.

That is **Q007**, and it is a gap in Northwestern's public record, not in this
repo's reading.

# 5. Something found and deliberately not fixed: `extra` never reaches a reader

While render-checking, the two new Northwestern items would not appear on the
page. They were in the data and valid. So was the pre-existing Bienen/ISP item
beside them.

`site/data/p-schools.js` gives each of the 30 schools an `extra: [...]` array.
**Nothing renders it.** `site/assets/js/app.js` contains no reader for the field
(its only `extra` is an unrelated parameter in a `stat()` helper), and the
strings are absent from the rendered DOM entirely — not hidden, not collapsed,
absent. The per-school `c:` cells, `judgment`, `plans` and `prompts` all render;
`extra` does not.

So a quantity of researched, cited, correctly-tagged per-school material has been
accumulating in the data since the first run and has never been on the site.
Purdue's "When you apply to Purdue, you apply for admission into a specific
major", its full review-factor list, Northwestern's Bienen and ISP extras,
Michigan's and Williams's extras — all of it.

**Not fixed this run, on purpose.** Rendering it means deciding where it goes on
an already dense page, how it interacts with 「只看官方來源」and 「摘要模式」,
and whether some items belong on `insights.html` instead. That is a design
decision on the maintainer's page, made with a budget to verify it, not a change
to drop in at the end of a watchdog run with the merge already blocked. Where a
fact mattered this run, it went into a field that does render — Purdue's letters
policy into `teach`, Michigan's and Williams's rules into the major-level
section, Princeton's quote onto the insights page.

It is logged as the next real unit of work and is the single highest-value
change available to the site, because the content already exists and is already
verified.
