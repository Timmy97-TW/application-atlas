# Reader-side reality / 讀者端的實際狀況

Scope: published reading processes in each office's own words, stated reading times, committee structure, Common Data Set (CDS) section C7 for all 30 schools, the SFFA v. Harvard trial record on Harvard's reading procedures (2014–2019 era), statements on supplementary materials and links, and UC comprehensive review.

Method and limits:
- Only what an admissions office said in its own voice is `official`. Court opinions are classed `secondary` as the task specifies, even though they quote Harvard's internal documents.
- Many blog posts cited here are old. The MIT blog marks pre-2020 staff posts as "archived" and says they are not current policy. Each post's date is given, and nothing old is presented as current practice.
- Web search ran out of budget during this run. Sources were reached by direct URL and by following links on official pages. Gaps this caused are listed under "Gaps" at the end.

摘要（中文）：本筆記只收錄招生辦公室自己說過的話（官方部落格、podcast、說明頁、CDS），以及法院判決書中對 Harvard 2014–2019 年閱卷程序的描述。凡是關於「讀者實際怎麼讀」而招生辦公室沒有說過的，一律標 `[推論]`。

## 1. Published reading processes, in the offices' own words

### 1.1 MIT
- [事實] MIT's current "Our selection process" page says an application "will first be read by a senior admissions officer", strong applications are then "evaluated by additional admissions officers" who summarise them for the selection committee, and "At least a dozen people will significantly discuss and debate an application" before it is admitted. Committee groups include "admissions staff and faculty members". (S06)
- [事實] The same page says there are "no quotas by school, state, or region" and that MIT does not consider "legacy/alumni relations". (S06)
- [事實] The page does not state how long a read takes or how votes are counted. (S06, checked 2026-11-07)
- [事實] An archived 2019 staff post describes committee days of "8-9 hours evaluating applications in the same room with the same people", "multiple committees simultaneously", and decisions reached through "discussion and consensus". It says MIT is "not traditionally regionalized". The page carries MIT's own notice that it is archived and not current policy. (S08)

### 1.2 Yale (Inside the Yale Admissions Office podcast, episode 2, "Committee", 21 May 2020)
- [事實] The area admissions officer reads the files first. The officer's notes and an outside reader's notes appear on the committee "slate". (S09)
- [事實] Committee size and make-up: five members. They are the area officer, a senior officer as chair, often another officer, a faculty member, and a dean from Yale College. (S09)
- [事實] Voting is anonymous, by clicker. "For an applicant to be admitted, four out of the five votes need to be an admit vote." (S09)
- [事實] Schedule: "two committees running all day, five days a week for six weeks" in regular decision. A session runs about nine hours. Discussion of one applicant "can take 30 seconds or 30 minutes." (S09)
- [事實] A speaker gave five minutes per applicant as a hypothetical, to show the scale for more than 35,000 applicants. It was not stated as a measured reading time. (S09)
- [事實] The hosts say the views in the podcast are their own and "don't necessarily represent those of Yale University" (S10). This disclaimer applies to everything cited from the podcast.

### 1.3 Georgia Tech (admission blog)
- [事實] In 2017 GT moved first-year review to "Committee Based Evaluation" (CBE). A permanent staff member (the "driver") was paired with a seasonal reader (the "passenger"). Reading ran 8:30 a.m.–2:30 p.m. daily to limit reader fatigue. At peak, each pair was expected to read 50 applications a day, about 3,000 a week across teams. (S11, 12 Oct 2017)
- [事實] The same post says time per application "increased, not decreased" under CBE. Staff read both pages of the activities list because they had missed significant activities listed at the end. All essays are read, though quickly. Counselors are told to put the most important information first. (S11)
- [事實] Norming: before reading, staff jointly reviewed sets of in-state, out-of-state and international files and discussed them to align their judgments. (S11)
- [事實] During the pandemic GT replaced CBE with a "Linear Application Review Process" (LARP). Each applicant gets two sequential reviews, one by a reader who knows the applicant's region and one by a reader who specialises in content (honors, activities, essays). Small committees of 2–3 people then discuss files. The office reported "500-1,300 combined files reviewed in a day" and said there was no minutes-per-file target. (S12, 10 Sep 2021)
- [事實] GT says reviewers use the school profile, school report or transcript to understand the GPA range typical at a given high school, and that "in holistic review, context is everything." (S13, 7 Jan 2022)
- [事實] I did not read the book by Georgia Tech's admissions dean (The Truth about College Admission) or excerpts from it in this run. Not stated (checked 2026-11-07).

### 1.4 Duke, Johns Hopkins, Penn, Rice, Swarthmore
- [事實] Duke's "What We Look For" page says applications are read and then discussed by the "Admissions Committee". Review is "guided initially by our assessment of five primary factors": rigor, grades, recommendations, extracurriculars and essays. The page states no rating scale and no reading time. (S14)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins groups its criteria as Academic Character, Impact & Initiative, and Overall Match, and says which application parts it "typically" finds each in. It does not state the number of readers or a reading time. (S15)
- [事實] Penn, Rice and Swarthmore: in this run I found no official page or blog post describing their reading model (committee-based or "shared" review, number of reads, time per file). Not stated (checked 2026-11-07). Penn's old "what-penn-looks-for" URL returns 404.
- [推論] The "committee-based evaluation" label circulates in the profession, but among the 30 schools only Georgia Tech has documented it in its own words, in 2017 (S11). GT then reported dropping it in favour of LARP (S12). A reader should not assume any other school in the 30 uses CBE without an official statement.

### 1.5 Caltech (faculty read files)
- [事實] Caltech says: "faculty read applications. In fact, every student admitted to Caltech receives a positive review from a professor." It also says "After the staff review all completed applications, 20+ faculty do a full read" (S22). The page quotes the chair of the First-Year Admissions Committee, a professor.
- [事實] Caltech's "How we review" page says: "No single grade, test score, course, essay, activity, award, or recommendation determines an admission decision". It names three qualities: Capacity for Caltech Rigor, Scholarly Character, and Scientific Drive. It says activities are not evaluated "by counting activities or rewarding a particular collection of programs, competitions, research positions, or leadership titles." (S23)

### 1.6 Harvard (current, in its own voice)
- [事實] "Each application to Harvard is read with great care" and "Harvard admission officers review all materials that an applicant submits." The page states no number of readers. (S16)

### 1.7 UC campuses
- [事實] UCLA: "All applications are read twice, in their entirety, by professionally trained readers." The reader "determines a comprehensive score". Criteria "carry no pre-assigned weights". UCLA cites "the extensive reader training", and admissions managers run "multiple checks for consistency". (S02)
- [事實] UC Berkeley: holistic review is defined as evaluation "where no one piece of information is weighted more heavily over another". Race, ethnicity, gender and religion are excluded from the criteria. (S03, S04)

## 3. SFFA v. Harvard: Harvard's reading procedures in the trial record (2014–2019 era, not now)

What these sources are: the September 2019 District of Massachusetts opinion (Document 672) and the 2023 Supreme Court opinion. Both are court documents describing Harvard's process in the period litigated. The district court says its summaries "reflect the Class of 2018 reading procedures" and that procedures for the classes of 2014 through 2019 "do not differ in material respects". The underlying "reading procedures" exhibits (PX1, PX723, DX5) are Harvard documents, but here they are seen only through the court's quotations, so they are classed `secondary`. None of this describes Harvard's current process. Race-conscious rating ended with the 2023 ruling, and Harvard's current CDS (C04) is a separate, later self-description.

- [事實] Applications were divided into geographic "dockets" by high school location. Each docket subcommittee had a senior officer as docket chair plus three to six other officers. Each member read all applications from a subset of the docket's high schools, so the same reader saw every applicant from a given school. (S26, pp. 18)
- [事實] Reading procedures were "updated on an annual basis". (S26, p. 18)
- [事實] Readers assigned an overall rating, four profile ratings (academic, extracurricular, athletic, personal), and at least three "school support" ratings for the teacher and counselor letters. They "may also rate the strength of any additional recommendations". (S26, p. 18)
- [事實] The scale ran mainly 1–4, with 1 strongest. Ratings of 5 and 6 indicated weakness or special circumstances. Officers could add "+" and "–" (2+ > 2 > 2–). Ratings were "preliminary" starting points for the committee. (S26, p. 19)
- [事實] Academic 1 meant summa cum laude potential with "possible evidence of original scholarship". The academic rating could draw on "any submitted academic work". (S26, p. 19)
- [事實] Extracurricular 1 meant "national-level, professional or other truly unusual achievement". 2 meant strong school-level contributions or local/regional recognition. 3 meant "solid participation but without special distinction". (S26, p. 20)
- [事實] Personal-rating descriptors in the litigated cycles were 1 "outstanding", 2 "very strong", 3 "generally positive", 4 "bland or somewhat negative or immature". For the class of 2023 (2018 cycle) the procedures were "overhauled". They then stated that race "should not be considered in assigning the personal rating" and listed qualities such as courage, leadership, maturity, resiliency and judgment. (S26, pp. 20–21)
- [事實] School support: 1 "strikingly unusual support", 2 "very strong", 3 "above average positive", 4 "somewhat neutral or slightly negative". These ratings did not feed the profile ratings "in a formulaic manner". (S26, p. 21)
- [事實] The first reader began with a two-to-three-page "summary sheet" pre-populated with school, test scores, GPA, class rank and other data. The reader passed promising files to the docket chair, who often served as second reader. Files not passed on stayed available for later discussion. (S26, pp. 22–23)
- [事實] At docket subcommittee meetings, first readers acted as "advocate" for their applicants and presented them high school by high school. The full committee had 40 members, required a majority vote for tentative admission, and ended with a "lop" stage that trimmed the list using four data points. (S26, pp. 23–24; S27, pp. 2–3)
- [事實] Alumni interviewers rated academic, personal and overall qualities on roughly the same 1–4 criteria. Interviews generally lasted 45 minutes to an hour. Interviewers were told not to advise applicants on their chances. (S26, pp. 14–15)
- [推論] Two structural points from this record may matter to an applicant from an unfamiliar school abroad: the same reader saw every applicant from a given high school, and readers built familiarity with each school's "grading practices, academic rigor, and recommendation styles" (S26, p. 18). That makes the school profile and counselor letter the reader's main calibration tools. This is inferred from a 2014–2019 Harvard procedure, and no current office has said it in these terms.

## 5. UC comprehensive review

- [事實] UC lists 13 comprehensive-review factors (S01). In brief:
  (1) GPA in A-G courses;
  (2) courses beyond the A-G minimum;
  (3) UC-approved honors, AP, IB HL and transferable college courses;
  (4) identification as top 9% of the school (ELC);
  (5) quality of the senior-year programme;
  (6) academic performance relative to opportunities available;
  (7) outstanding performance in specific subject areas;
  (8) "Outstanding work in one or more special projects in any academic field of study";
  (9) recent marked improvement;
  (10) special talents, achievements and awards;
  (11) "Completion of special projects undertaken in the context of your high school curriculum";
  (12) academic accomplishments in light of life experiences and special circumstances;
  (13) "Location of your secondary school and residence."
- [事實] "While all UC campuses use the same factors to evaluate applications, they often apply these factors differently." "Each UC campus evaluates each application without knowing the status of the same application at another campus." (S01)
- [事實] The UC-wide page says nothing about reader training or the number of readers (S01). UCLA states two full reads and "extensive reader training" (S02). Berkeley's page says no transcripts or portfolios are requested and other supporting materials are not considered (S03).
- [事實] Regents Policy 2110, "Augmented Review" (approved 2017, amended 2022), lets campuses ask up to 15% of applicants near the margin for more information. This can be a questionnaire, seventh-semester grades, or "Up to two letters of recommendation or other third-party input". (S05)
- [推論] Factors 8 and 11 name "special projects". In the UC system, which accepts no uploads, the only places such projects can appear are the activities/awards entries, the PIQs and Additional Comments, plus augmented-review letters if a campus requests them. This follows from S01 and S03 together.

## Non-obvious findings (each cited)

1. [事實] Caltech says every admitted student "receives a positive review from a professor" and that "20+ faculty do a full read" after staff review. Among the 30, this is the only explicit statement found that faculty read whole first-year files. (S22)
2. [事實] Yale's committee (2020 description) has five voters including a faculty member and a dean, and admission needs 4 of 5 anonymous clicker votes. (S09)
3. [事實] MIT states that at least a dozen people discuss an application before admission. (S06)
4. [事實] Georgia Tech is the only school of the 30 found documenting "Committee Based Evaluation" in its own words (2017), with 50 files per pair per day at peak. It later switched to a two-reader sequential model (LARP) because seasonal readers' schedules varied. Review models change, and published descriptions go stale. (S11, S12)
5. [事實] GT's 2017 post says staff had been missing significant activities placed at the end of the activities list, and changed practice to read both pages. It advises listing the most significant activities first. (S11)
6. [事實] Columbia states that links placed outside the designated research-abstract field "will not be reviewed". (S20)
7. [事實] Yale officers said in 2020 that they "rarely review" outside links. (S10)
8. [事實] Princeton will put arts materials outside its categories "in your file" but says it cannot guarantee time to review them, and asks applicants not to link personal websites in the arts supplement. (S17)
9. [事實] Princeton requires a graded written paper and says it does not want science research for it. A research paper therefore cannot double as this requirement. (S18)
10. [事實] Princeton arts-supplement content "will only be read by the arts faculty" unless it is also submitted as additional information, so information placed there may never reach the admission officer. (S17)
11. [事實] Yale's faculty review of arts supplements is "blind": the faculty member sees only the work, not essays or transcript. STEM supplements are reviewed first by admissions officers. (S10)
12. [事實] Berkeley states it does not consider portfolios, resumes or other supporting materials. Under UC Regents Policy 2110, up to two third-party letters can enter only if a campus requests augmented review, which is capped at 15% of applicants. (S03, S05)
13. [事實] Caltech requires a mentor letter for each research submission that speaks to the applicant's "specific contributions". Harvard asks for a list of collaborators. Brown asks whether the work was done independently or as part of a team. Three offices independently ask for attribution of individual contribution. (S24, S16, S21)
14. [事實] Only about 42% of Caltech's admitted students submitted research, by Caltech's own figure. (S24, S28)
15. [事實] Harvard's 2014–2019 procedures had readers rate "any additional recommendations" and let the academic rating draw on "any submitted academic work", so extra material had a defined place in the ratings at that time. (S26, pp. 18–19)
16. [事實] Under those procedures one reader read all applicants from a given high school, in order to learn that school's grading and recommendation styles. (S26, p. 18)
17. [事實] In 2014–2019 the Harvard personal rating of 3 was "generally positive" and 4 was "bland or somewhat negative or immature". (S26, p. 20)
18. [事實] The 2025–26 CDS template has removed the "Racial/ethnic status" row from C7 across all files read. (C01–C31)
19. [事實] Harvard's 2025–26 CDS marks no factor "Very Important" or "Important". MIT's marks only character "Very Important". (C04, C01)
20. [事實] Olin's 2023–24 CDS marks Interview and Level of interest "Very Important", the only school in the table to do either. (C18)
21. [事實] Carnegie Mellon marks Volunteer work "Very Important". No other school in the table does. (C13)
22. [事實] UW Seattle and Georgia Tech mark State residency "Very Important", and UC San Diego marks it "Important". (C28, C14, C26)
23. [事實] The only stated per-hour reading rate found in an official or court source is UNC's "roughly five applications per hour", from the 2023 Supreme Court opinion. UNC is not among the 30. (S27)
24. [推論] Reading speeds and committee formats are published as dated snapshots (GT 2017 and 2021, Yale 2020, MIT 2019 archived). Any site statement about "how long a reader spends" should carry the year and the school, and should not be generalised.
