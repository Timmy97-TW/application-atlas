# Admissions offices in their own voice / 招生辦公室親口說的話

Scope: what the admissions offices of the 30 target schools have published themselves about eight application parts. The sources are official pages, FAQs, counselor pages, blogs and podcast transcripts. This note is a companion to `2026-11-07_reader-side-reality_v1.md`, which covers reading process and committee structure. Here the question is narrower: what each office tells applicants to do, or not do, with each part.

Method and limits:
- All sources are `official` (an admissions office's own site, blog, FAQ or podcast) unless the Sources table says otherwise. Access date for every source: 2026-11-07.
- Student bloggers and student interviewers are labelled **(student)**. Their posts are hosted by the office but are not policy.
- Staff are named by role only. No staff names appear in this note.
- Post dates are given where the page shows one. **Pre-2020 posts are flagged ⚠pre-2020.** MIT marks its pre-2020 blog posts "archived" and says they are not current policy. Several undated pages are evergreen policy pages. "undated" means the page shows no date.
- The Yale podcast hosts say the views are their own and "don't necessarily represent those of Yale University" (see the reader-side note). That caveat applies to every Yale podcast item below.
- Web search and direct fetch were both used. Some official sites refused direct fetch (TLS errors or HTTP 403/405): Princeton, Northwestern, Columbia, Michigan, Williams, Amherst staff page, Purdue. For these, the wording comes from the search engine's excerpt of the official page. The Sources table marks them **index-excerpt**. They should be re-checked in a browser before any wording goes on the site.
- Quotes are each under 15 words. Everything else is paraphrase.
- Tags: `[事實]` = an admissions office said it (cited). `[推論]` = my synthesis across sources. Reader behaviour that no office stated is never tagged `[事實]`.

中文摘要：本筆記整理 30 校招生辦公室「自己說過」關於活動表、主文書與補充文書、推薦信、Additional Information、面試、demonstrated interest、國際生、研究／作品集補充資料的話。每條附來源編號、日期與角色（學生部落客另行標註）。2020 年前的舊文標示 ⚠pre-2020。每個主題末尾的 `[推論]` 是跨校歸納，不是任何招生辦公室的說法。

## 1. The activities list / 活動表

- [事實] MIT (apply page, undated): "Please use our form, not a resume, to list your activities." Space is limited to four entries; pick "the four that mean the most to you". (O01)
- [事實] MIT (what we look for, undated): "We don't expect applicants to do a million things." (O02)
- [事實] MIT (parents & educators page, undated): "We don't want a laundry list of a million activities." (O04)
- [事實] MIT (prepare: activities, undated): choose activities that "delight, intrigue, and challenge you", not ones that look impressive. It names time spent on "family, work, or other obligations" as legitimate. (O18)
- [事實] MIT (staff blog, 29 Oct 2007, archived ⚠pre-2020): "long lists of activities and awards are most often not useful." Extra sheets help with "depth, not breadth." (O14)
- [事實] Harvard (application tips, updated 23 Sep 2026): "Filling out the grid is an act of prioritization." (O47)
- [事實] Harvard (same page): the office is more interested in "the quality of students' activities than their quantity". (O47)
- [事實] Penn (preparing your application: activities, undated): family responsibilities, summer activities and community experiences "all count as activities". (O61)
- [事實] Penn (same): "we don't value one kind of activity more than others!" (O61)
- [事實] Penn (same), practical tips: "Spell out any acronyms." "Include specific responsibilities for each activity." "Give us a sense of scope." (O61)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (Hopkins Insider, 2 Sep 2026; author shown only as a blog contributor): count "Anything that meaningfully takes up your time outside of class". (O25)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (same): "Be specific and use action words." The post rewrites a bare "Worked at a grocery store" into a fuller description as its example. (O25)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (same): "There isn't a magic number of activities we're looking for". (O25)
- [事實] Georgia Tech (admission blog, 18 Sep 2026; an admission professional focused on first-year recruitment): asks whether applicants considered adding "farm work or taking care of siblings". (O20)
- [事實] Georgia Tech (same): readers value "you earning a position of trust in your community". (O20)
- [事實] Georgia Tech (admission blog, 13 Oct 2020; staff section): "Focus on what you've contributed to your school, community, or family." (O21)
- [事實] Georgia Tech (application review page, undated): AI may help "construct your resume in the Activities portion" of the Common App. (O22)
- [事實] Rice (blog, admission officers, last updated 9 Aug 2023): "Provide detailed descriptions and quantify your impact within your activities." Work and family duties "are just as important as school activities". (O67)
- [事實] Olin (blog, admission staff, 11 Sep 2023): "Anything is an activity!" and "Quality over Quantity!" Describe the impact you made in each group. (O88)
- [事實] Duke (what we look for, undated): "we appreciate well-rounded and well-lopsided students". (O40)
- [事實] Yale (podcast, 1 Sep 2026, admissions officers): academic strength "should not be a reason to try to present a spike." (O37)
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 35, 25 Sep 2023): "you certainly do not need a passion project to get into Yale". (O34)
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 52, 27 Feb 2026): more important to see "you're building sustainable habits." (O38)
- [事實] Caltech (how we review, undated): "We do not evaluate these experiences by counting activities". (O55)
- [事實] CMU (admission consideration, undated): activities, jobs and family commitments "play a very important role in the admission process". (O75)
- [事實] UW (holistic review, undated): lists "Exercising significant responsibility in a family, community, employment situation" as a factor. (O83)
- [推論] Every office that addresses the list says the same three things: depth beats count; jobs and family care are activities; describe what you did, not your title. None publishes an ideal number of entries.
- [推論] Penn's tips (acronyms, scope, responsibilities) and Hopkins's before/after rewrite are the most concrete formatting guidance found. MIT's four-slot form shows the same preference built into the form itself.

## 2. Personal essay and supplements / 主文書與補充文書

### What offices say they want
- [事實] MIT (apply page, undated): the essays are "not a writing test". "Be honest, be open, be authentic." (O01)
- [事實] MIT (same): if you are "stressing or strategizing about what makes you 'look best'", "you're doing it wrong". (O01)
- [事實] MIT (be yourself page, undated): "College isn't a costume party; you're not supposed to come dressed as someone else." (O03)
- [事實] MIT (staff blog, 30 Jul 2012, edited 2023, archived ⚠pre-2020): "Specificity, clarity, and brevity are your keys." "Keep it simple." (O17)
- [事實] MIT **(student)** (blog, 12 Nov 2015, archived ⚠pre-2020): "Don't apply as a fictional character." (O12)
- [事實] Princeton (dean of admission, blog on essays and AI, 2026, **index-excerpt**): "We are not looking for perfect. We're looking for authentic." (O58)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (Essays That Worked, Class of 2030 essays, page undated): the office's main advice is to "be original". Essays were nominated by the admissions committee. (O24)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (Hopkins Insider, 3 Aug 2026, office voice): "Focus on the process, rather than the outcome, in your essay." (O27)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (same): "You don't need to be the hero of the story!" (O27)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (Hopkins Insider, 7 Nov 2023): each part should "complement, rather than repeat, other sections." (O26)
- [事實] Caltech (how we review, undated): "We are less interested in polished performance". (O55)
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 35, 25 Sep 2023): "we are not looking for pieces of publishable work". (O34)
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 29, 9 Nov 2022): "We learn a lot more about your achievements from your essays". (O35)
- [事實] Yale (podcast, 1 Sep 2026): "These essays are not meant to be academic in their nature." (O37)
- [事實] Georgia Tech (blog, 13 Oct 2020, staff): "Essays should be personal and detailed." (O21)
- [事實] UW (writing section, undated): readers want to "see the person behind the transcripts and the numbers." There is no preferred prompt. (O82)
- [事實] Olin (blog, admission staff, 7 Sep 2022): "Through your essays, we get to hear your voice in the application." (O90)

### Common mistakes named by the offices
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 35, 25 Sep 2023): "Don't read too many examples of successful college essays." (O34)
- [事實] Yale (same): "Standing out is not the goal." (O34)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (3 Aug 2026): "try to avoid generic language or takeaways." The essay should not be about "winning an argument". (O27)
- [事實] Harvey Mudd (FAQ, undated): "Avoid using your essay to list your activities and achievements." (O91)
- [事實] MIT **(student)** (2015, archived ⚠pre-2020): "Don't indulge in exquisite words." (O12)
- [事實] Penn (writing page, undated): "Be sure you're answering the question or prompt being asked." Do not rush, so you have time to revise. (O62)
- [事實] UW (writing section): "Write like it matters, not like you're texting." (O82)

### Supplements and "why us"
- [事實] Penn (writing page): "Show us why you're applying here." (O62)
- [事實] Rice (blog, admission counselor, 18 Jun 2025): move "beyond general descriptions towards specific experiences, interests and thoughts". (O70)
- [事實] Rice (same): writing about location or campus size "doesn't necessarily reflect deep exploration". (O70)
- [事實] Rice (blog, admission officers, updated 9 Aug 2023): "You can't just copy and paste your responses" across schools. (O67)
- [事實] Duke (FAQ, undated, wording partly **index-excerpt**): applicants should show "demonstrated knowledge" through the application rather than visits or contacts. (O41)
- [事實] Michigan (blog and selection pages, **index-excerpt**): the supplemental questions are where applicants show why Michigan; reusing one essay everywhere lacks "the personal touch". (O79)
- [事實] Cornell (college pages, **index-excerpt**): applicants can state fit "through specific essay questions in the application". (O74)
- [事實] CMU (admission consideration, undated): for the third short answer, "Tell us, don't show us (no websites please)." (O75)
- [事實] UC (PIQ guide, 2026 PDF, **index-excerpt**): readers "cannot click on links or search for your accomplishment online." All PIQ choices get equal consideration. (O80)
- [事實] Georgia Tech (application review page, undated): on AI, "your ultimate submission should be your own." (O22)
- [推論] Two kinds of supplement advice recur. "Why us" answers must be specific to that school; Rice, Penn, Duke and Michigan all say so. Personal essays should be about process and reflection rather than achievement; Hopkins, Caltech, Yale and Princeton all say so.
- [推論] Three offices tell applicants to stop optimising: MIT ("doing it wrong"), Yale ("Standing out is not the goal") and Princeton ("not looking for perfect"). That suggests over-engineered essays are a pattern they see often. The offices do not say how often, so this stays an inference.

## 3. Recommendations / 推薦信

### What makes a letter useful
- [事實] MIT (how to write good letters, office voice, undated): letters are "most helpful when they are specific and storied." (O05)
- [事實] MIT (same): "glowing superlatives" help only "if they are backed up with examples." (O05)
- [事實] MIT (same): letters should "go beyond a student's grades and academic performance." (O05)
- [事實] MIT (same): a vague letter suggests "this teacher did not give much effort to the recommendation." (O05)
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 9, 13 Oct 2020): "the specifics are so much more powerful than the superlatives." (O33)
- [事實] Yale (same): "Pick some teachers who know you and who like you." (O33)
- [事實] Yale (podcast, 1 Sep 2026): "Don't underestimate the power of these recommendations." (O37)
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 52, 27 Feb 2026): about meeting your recommender, "Set up the meeting. Go there, even if it's 15 minutes." (O38)
- [事實] Caltech (writing advice for recommenders, undated): asks "Who is this student when the work becomes difficult?" (O54)
- [事實] Caltech (same): asks for evidence of perseverance, "such as a failed lab trial". Letters from a class with a strong writing component are important. (O54)
- [事實] Georgia Tech (blog, admission counselor, 5 Sep 2018 ⚠pre-2020): "A recommendation letter should highlight something new." (O23)
- [事實] Georgia Tech (blog, 13 Oct 2020, staff section): "Good recommendations showcase your character/compliment your story." (O21)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (Hopkins Insider, 26 Apr 2026): "Prioritize those who know you best." (O28)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (counselor letter samples, undated): the sample letters explain school limits, such as a cap on AP courses per year, to give context. (O30)
- [事實] CMU (admission consideration, undated): the teacher letter should come from "the teacher who knows you the best", not necessarily one in your intended field. (O75, wording via search excerpt of same page)
- [事實] Rice (blog, admission officers, 2023): recommends a teacher related to "your major of interest". (O67)
- [事實] Stanford (forms page, undated): "Letters from classroom teachers are strongly preferred." (O49)

### Extra letters and Other Recommender
- [事實] MIT (letters page, undated): an optional supplemental letter is accepted, but "Most applicants and most admitted students do not submit any supplemental recommendations." (O06)
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 9, 2020): "We do not need a third teacher. We only want two." "More is not necessarily better in this case". (O33)
- [事實] Brown (supplementary materials page, undated): the required letters "provide all we need to make a thoughtful, informed admission decision." (O72)
- [事實] Stanford (forms page): one Other Recommender may write if they can "provide new insights about you." (O49)
- [事實] Stanford (after you apply page, undated): "We only accept required documents." (O50)
- [事實] Rice (FAQ, undated): "A supplemental letter is not required or expected." (O68)
- [事實] Duke (apply page, undated): will accept "one additional letter of recommendation". If you are not offered an interview, you may still send one optional Personal Recommendation. (O43, O42)
- [事實] Harvey Mudd (FAQ): advises submitting at most one additional recommendation. (O91)
- [事實] Harvard (application requirements, undated): extra letters may be sent after applying through the personalised link in the confirmation email. (O46)
- [事實] Columbia (FAQ, **index-excerpt**): extra letters are considered only from a research supervisor or college-course instructor; other extras are discouraged. (O73)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (FAQ, undated): extra recommendations may be uploaded as optional material. Waitlisted students should not send additional letters. (O29)
- [事實] UC (counselor pages, **index-excerpt**): letters are not required and should not be submitted unless a campus or major asks. (O81)
- [事實] Illinois (first-year FAQ, undated): "No, we don't accept letter of recommendation or other unsolicited materials." Unsolicited letters are removed from the file before review. (O86)
- [事實] UT Austin (review page, undated): recommendations are reviewed "although not required". (O84) The UT FAQ, **index-excerpt**, says up to two letters and encourages sources outside the high school. (O85)
- [推論] Most offices say directly that an extra letter is not needed. None says that more letters hurt, except Illinois, which removes them, and UC, which tells applicants not to send them. An extra letter is worth sending only when the writer adds evidence the required letters cannot, such as a research mentor. Stanford ("new insights") and Columbia (research supervisor) say this most plainly.
- [推論] Three offices give writers a test for a strong letter: MIT, Caltech and Georgia Tech. Each asks for observed episodes rather than adjectives. An applicant can pass these pages to a teacher without editorialising.

## 4. Additional Information section / 補充說明欄

- [事實] MIT (apply page, undated): the application ends with "one final, open-ended, additional-information text box". (O01)
- [事實] MIT (FAQ, undated): "there are designated spaces to provide additional context". (O10)
- [事實] Georgia Tech (blog, 13 Oct 2020, staff section): "It's okay to leave this section blank." (O21)
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 35, 25 Sep 2023): "please don't write an extra essay there". (O34)
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 29, 9 Nov 2022): using it for a school-level award is "absolutely appropriate". (O35)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (Hopkins Insider, 7 Nov 2023): use it for content that "doesn't fit in the essays or activities list." (O26)
- [事實] UW (writing section, undated): suggested uses are "Unusual academic progression", "Course availability issues", and academic work shown nowhere else. (O82)
- [事實] Stanford (FAQ, **index-excerpt**): supplementary materials are not accepted; applicants may use Additional Information to elaborate on academics, activities, honours or background. (O53)
- [事實] Harvey Mudd (FAQ): applicants are not disadvantaged for submitting nothing extra. (O91)
- [事實] Penn (writing page, undated): the stated purpose of the optional spaces is for applicants "To be your own best advocate". (O62)
- [事實] MIT (staff blog, 2007, archived ⚠pre-2020): "None of the below is required or even expected." (O14)
- [推論] The offices agree on the use: context and facts with no other place, such as course availability, unusual progression, a school-level award, or a disruption. They agree on the misuse: a second essay. Georgia Tech's "okay to leave this section blank" is the clearest permission found.

## 5. Interviews / 面試

- [事實] MIT (interview page, undated): if no interview can be offered, "it will be waived, and your application will not be adversely affected." (O07)
- [事實] MIT (same): prepare "stories or examples that will give your interviewer a vivid sense" of you. Interviews typically last an hour. (O07)
- [事實] Yale (interviews page, undated): "Interviews are not required, and many successful applicants are not interviewed." (O36)
- [事實] Yale (same): "Interviews are by invitation only." "All Yale interviews are evaluative." (O36)
- [事實] Yale (same): "Not receiving an interview invitation is not an indication that your application is not competitive." (O36)
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 35, 2023): "An interviewer is someone who didn't see the rest of the application." (O34)
- [事實] Yale (podcast, 1 Sep 2026): "We aren't asking the interviewer to evaluate your academic preparation." (O37)
- [事實] Harvard (international applicants page, undated): "The absence of an interview will not adversely affect your candidacy." (O44, O45)
- [事實] Stanford (interview page, undated): "Your application will be considered complete with or without an interview." Invitations follow "alumni availability in your high school area." (O51)
- [事實] Duke (alumni interviews page, undated): "not receiving an interview will not affect your chances of admission." (O42)
- [事實] Duke (same): international applicants may use InitialView, and applicants in China "are particularly encouraged" to. (O42)
- [事實] Princeton (application checklist, **index-excerpt**, page noted as several years old): interviews are optional, and the interviewer's comments are included in the review. (O59)
- [事實] Georgetown (alumni interview pages): interviews are "a key part of Georgetown's application process". (O97) The FAQ, **index-excerpt**, says all first-year applicants must interview unless none can be assigned in their area. A waived interview has no negative effect. (O97, O98)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (FAQ): "we do not offer interviews as part of our admissions process". (O29)
- [事實] Michigan (selection process, **index-excerpt**): no first-year interview, because of the volume of applications. (O79)
- [事實] Williams (FAQ, **index-excerpt**): no interviews in the admission process. (O96)
- [事實] Amherst (first-year page, **index-excerpt**): no personal interviews for first-year applicants, on or off campus. (O95)
- [事實] Swarthmore (request an interview page, undated): "Applicants who do not interview are not at a disadvantage." Interviews are open only to students at US high schools. International students "are strongly encouraged to submit a Video Response." (O93)
- [事實] Harvey Mudd (interviews page, undated): "All interviews are conducted online." International applicants are encouraged to use InitialView or Vericant. There are no fee waivers for those services. (O92)
- [事實] Rice (international page, undated): alumni and student interviews are "weighted equally in the evaluation process". Not being matched does not affect the decision. (O69)
- [事實] Rice **(student)** (senior interviewers' blog, updated 6 Dec 2023): "research Rice and know why you want to go to school here." (O71)
- [事實] Olin (what we look for, undated): Candidates' Weekend attendance "is required to be considered for admission". It has "evaluative components". Students outside the continental US interview virtually. (O89)
- [事實] Georgia Tech (application review page): lists "Interview for International Applicants" as a review factor without detail. (O22)
- [事實] CMU (requirements page, undated): an optional VIVA video assessment on InitialView's platform, for Engineering and Computer Science applicants only. (O76)
- [事實] Penn (Ask Penn, **index-excerpt**): international applicants are welcome to submit an InitialView interview. It does not replace English testing. (O65)
- [推論] Where interviews exist they are almost always "optional, no penalty if not offered". Olin is the exception (required Candidates' Weekend). Georgetown requires an interview where one can be assigned. For a Taiwan-based applicant, availability depends on volunteer supply, which Stanford and Georgetown both say outright.
- [推論] InitialView, and sometimes Vericant, is the official substitute named by Duke, Harvey Mudd, Penn, Georgetown, Caltech and Swarthmore (Swarthmore also offers its own video response). None says it is required for first-year applicants.

## 6. Demonstrated interest / 是否追蹤「表現興趣」

- [事實] MIT (about page, undated): "we don't consider demonstrated interest". (O11)
- [事實] MIT (dean's blog, 27 May 2020, archived): "we do not consider demonstrated interest as part of your application". (O15)
- [事實] Duke (FAQ, undated, **index-excerpt** for exact wording): demonstrated interest as most use the term "is not a plus factor". "Students don't need to visit the campus" (fetched). Contacting the office to show interest is discouraged. (O41)
- [事實] Yale (podcast ep. 52, 27 Feb 2026): "We do not track demonstrated interest at all." (O38)
- [事實] Johns Hopkins (FAQ): "we do not track demonstrated interest". (O29)
- [事實] Harvey Mudd (FAQ): "We assume that you've applied to Harvey Mudd" because you are interested; interest is not considered. (O91)
- [事實] Swarthmore (interview page): "Swarthmore does not practice 'demonstrated interest' in its application review process." (O93)
- [事實] Columbia (FAQ, **index-excerpt**): does not track demonstrated interest; no advantage from contacting the office. (O73)
- [事實] Cornell (visit page on a dev subdomain, **index-excerpt**): "Cornell University does not track demonstrated interest." Visiting is "not required nor expected". (O74)
- [事實] Northwestern (regional programming page, **index-excerpt**): interest is not treated as "a tally of touchpoints". (O77)
- [事實] Amherst (staff page, **index-excerpt**): does not track demonstrated interest; no reason to contact a particular dean. (O94)
- [事實] Rice (application philosophy, undated): never uses the phrase, but asks applicants to register or complete a prospective-student form. The supplement should say which "specific aspects of Rice excite you". "Knowledge about Rice" is listed among the factors. (O66)
- [事實] Georgia Tech (application review page): "evidence of your interest in this major is an indicator". The interest meant is in the major, not in the school. (O22)
- [事實] UT Austin (waitlist page, **index-excerpt**): continued-interest letters and other extras submitted while waitlisted "will not be accepted or reviewed." (O99)
- [推論] Among schools that address the question, the stated position is "not tracked" for MIT, Yale, Duke, Hopkins, Columbia, Cornell, Harvey Mudd, Swarthmore, Amherst and Northwestern. Rice is the clearest counter-example: it asks for a form registration and lists knowledge of Rice as a factor. Several offices (Duke, Cornell, Rice) say fit should show in the "why us" essay instead of in contact counts.
- [推論] Schools not found saying anything either way in this run: Stanford, Harvard, Princeton, Penn, Brown, CMU, Michigan, UIUC, Purdue, UC, UW, Williams, Olin, Georgetown. That is "not stated (checked 2026-11-07)". It is not evidence either way.

## 7. International applicants / 國際生

- [事實] MIT (international FAQ, undated): "MIT practices need-blind admissions." Almost one quarter of applications come from international students. (O13)
- [事實] MIT (first-year international page, undated): "we do not have caps or quotas for countries". (O08)
- [事實] MIT (international FAQ): all materials must be in English, and "you can't translate the material yourself." (O13)
- [事實] MIT **(student)** (blog, 25 Oct 2018, archived ⚠pre-2020): described MIT as one of "only five need-blind admissions schools". The current FAQ gives a different count, so the student's number should not be used. (O16)
- [事實] Harvard (international applicants page, undated): "Our admissions and financial aid processes are the same for all applicants". (O44)
- [事實] Harvard (same): a copy of the original document "should always accompany the translated version". A teacher may translate; the applicant may not. (O44)
- [事實] Harvard (same): non-US school documents "may differ substantially from that submitted by an American high school". (O44)
- [事實] Yale (international page, undated): applicants are considered "without regard to their ability to pay". Self-made translations are not accepted. (O39)
- [事實] Stanford (international page, undated): for international citizens, an aid request "will be a factor in our admission evaluation." (O52)
- [事實] Stanford (same): "applicants are reviewed in the context and country where they attend high school." (O52)
- [事實] Stanford (same): "Transcripts and teacher recommendations must be translated into English." (O52)
- [事實] Caltech (international page, undated): "need-aware for international students" because aid funds are limited. (O57)
- [事實] Caltech (same): "We will not review your application if any part is not translated in English." The transcript does not need converting to US grades. (O57)
- [事實] Penn (Ask Penn, modified 1 Aug 2025): "Penn is need-aware for international students who are not from Canada and Mexico." (O64)
- [事實] Penn (school profile page, undated): officers use profiles to "evaluate each applicant within their context". Profiles should show "what classes and extra-curricular opportunities were available". (O63)
- [事實] Duke (apply page, undated): foreign citizens must apply for aid "in the original first-year application". The same page states the US-citizen no-disadvantage rule, which implies a different rule for foreign citizens. (O43)
- [事實] Rice (international page): international applicants are considered on a "need-aware basis". (O69)
- [事實] Rice (same): non-English documents need an official translation from the school or an "authorized/official translator in the country of study." (O69)
- [事實] Harvey Mudd (FAQ): "We are need aware in the admission process for international students". (O91)
- [事實] Georgetown (international FAQ, undated): admitted international students who request aid are considered for "a limited number of need-based scholarship awards". (O98)
