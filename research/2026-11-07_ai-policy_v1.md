# C4 — AI policy / AI 使用政策

**中文摘要** [事實] Common App 的 Fraud Policy 把「將 AI 平台的 substantive content or output 當成自己的原創作品」列為 fraud（經 Brown 與 Harvard 官方頁引述，S1, S2）。UC 的 Statement of Application Integrity（2023 年 8 月更新）允許用 generative AI 協助 readability，但內容與最終文字必須是學生自己的，且 UC 可要求 authentication（S5）。30 校中，本次在官方頁直接讀到校方 AI 說明的有 17 校；5 校只能經第三方轉述確認；4 校官方頁查無 AI 文字；3 校官方頁本次無法讀取、僅有第三方「查無政策」紀錄；Purdue 未能取得。[推論] 多數政策劃的線是「內容與文字的來源」而非「是否碰過 AI」，而 Penn 的「能否在對談中說明你如何寫出來」與 Olin 的現場面試／團體活動，是本次唯一直接指向口頭說明或現場觀察的官方文字。

**English summary** [事實] Common App's Fraud Policy defines as fraud intentionally presenting as one's own work "the substantive content or output of an artificial intelligence platform" (quoted on Brown's official page, S1). UC permits generative AI "to assist with readability" but requires that content and final text be the student's own, and reserves the right to request authentication (S5). Of the 30 schools, 17 have a school-specific AI statement read directly on an official page this run; 5 are confirmed only through secondary reporting; 4 have no AI text on the official pages checked; 3 could not be read officially and have only a secondary "no policy found" record; Purdue could not be retrieved.

Conventions: every bullet is tagged. "Read" means fetched with WebFetch on 2026-11-07. Quotes are kept under 15 words.

## 1. Application platforms

### 1.1 Common App

- [事實] Common App publishes a Fraud Policy at commonapp.org/fraud-policy and as a PDF; on 2026-11-07 only the section headings (Overview, Scope, Fraud, … Actions Subsequent to a Fraud Determination) could be extracted from either; the body text was not retrievable (S3, S4). The text below is therefore taken from member schools that quote it on their own official pages.
- [事實] Brown quotes the Fraud Policy definition: fraud includes intentionally misrepresenting as one's own original work "(2) the substantive content or output of an artificial intelligence platform, technology, or algorithm" (S1). Harvard quotes the same clause (S2).
- [事實] The affirmation the applicant signs, as quoted by Brown and Harvard: the applicant certifies that all information submitted "is my own work, factually true, and honestly presented" (S1, S2).
- [事實] Per Brown's quotation, the applicant also acknowledges possible "admission revocation, expulsion, or revocation of course credit, grades, and degree" if the certification is false (S1).
- [事實] When the AI clause was added: not stated on any official page read (checked 2026-11-07, S3, S4). A secondary source says August 2023 without citing a document (S43); other secondary sources in search results said 2024. Treat the date as unverified.
- [事實] Whether Common App itself runs AI-detection software: not stated (checked 2026-11-07, S3). One secondary source says it does not, without citing a Common App document (S43).
- [事實] MIT does not use Common App, so Common App's affirmation does not apply to MIT applicants (S35, secondary; MIT's own application is described on S17).

### 1.2 University of California (UC application, PIQs)

- [事實] UC's Statement of Application Integrity, "Last updated August 2023", says PIQ responses must "be independently written by the student" and reflect the student's own writing style (S5).
- [事實] The same statement permits advice "including the use of generative artificial intelligence software to assist with readability", but "content and final written text must be their own" (S5).
- [事實] UC "may request authentication of the content or writing as the student's" and conducts regular screenings (S5).
- [事實] The statement also requires that awards, honors, activities and work "must be true and verifiable by an independent party" (S5).
- [事實] Penalties listed: up to denial, withdrawal of offer, registration cancellation, expulsion, or degree revocation (S5).
- [事實] UC's first-year PIQ page says answers should be "written by you alone" and has no AI-specific text (S6).
- [事實] In a UC news article dated 2025-10-23, UC Davis's executive director of undergraduate admissions says AI "is a tool that you can use" for PIQ help, and that the submission "better be your own creation" (S7).
- [事實] UC states it "doesn't use artificial intelligence in its application review process" and treats a fully AI-generated answer as equivalent to academic dishonesty (S8; article dated 2026-10-07, originally 2024).
- [事實] These statements are system-wide; UC Berkeley, UCLA and UC San Diego publish no separate undergraduate AI policy found in this run (secondary cross-check S35, S44).

### 1.3 Coalition on Scoir

- [事實] Coalition on Scoir's own AI or fraud language: not stated in any source read (checked 2026-11-07). A secondary source asserts "substantively similar fraud language" without citation (S43). Gap; not verified.
- [事實] Swarthmore's guidance tells applicants to review the Coalition, Common App and QuestBridge policies, which "may have their own" AI rules (S14).

## 3. [推論] What these policies imply for process-based, timestamped, orally defended evidence versus polished written artifacts

Everything in this section is inference from the facts above, not a statement by any admissions office. The maintainer has no admissions-office experience (CLAUDE.md rule 3).

- [推論] The line most policies draw is about the source of content and final text, not about whether AI was touched (Common App "substantive content", S1; UC "content and final written text must be their own", S5; Caltech, Cornell, Swarthmore prohibitions on AI outlining/drafting, S9, S13, S14). A written artifact therefore carries an authorship question that the artifact alone cannot answer.
- [推論] Three official texts point to ways authorship gets checked outside the essay: UC's right to request authentication (S5), Harvard's contact about authenticity (S2), and Penn's explain-in-conversation standard (S12). Evidence that an applicant can reconstruct and explain (drafts, lab notebooks, commit histories, dated logs, an oral account) fits these checks more directly than a finished text does. No school says it asks for such records; this is a fit, not a stated preference.
- [推論] UC's requirement that activities be "verifiable by an independent party" (S5) and Brown's counselor verification (S1) suggest that claims tied to a named third party (judge, organiser, supervisor) are the kind of record these offices already have a procedure for. This links to unit C3.
- [推論] Cornell's ban on AI creating portfolio works (S13) means a portfolio is inside the same authorship rule as an essay. Process documentation for a portfolio piece would be the applicant's own evidence of authorship if questioned; Cornell does not say it asks for it.
- [推論] Olin's required interview, group exercise and design challenge (S30) are the only official example among the 30 of live observation as a standing part of evaluation. For the other 29, an oral defence of work exists only where an interview happens to exist (optional or alumni-run) or where a school contacts the applicant.
- [推論] Policies differ on translation: Caltech and Cornell prohibit AI translation of essays (S9, S13). For an applicant whose first language is not English, a record that was originally produced in another language would need to be re-written by the applicant, not machine-translated, to stay inside these rules.
- [推論] Duke's removal of numeric essay ratings, with AI cited as a reason (S33, secondary), is one reported case of a school lowering the weight of polished prose. It is one school and one secondary report; it does not show a trend.
- [假設] That a timestamped record would be read as stronger evidence than an essay by any particular reader is an assumption. No source read says so.

## 4. Non-obvious findings

1. [事實] UC explicitly permits generative AI "to assist with readability" and dated that text August 2023 (S5) — earlier and more permissive in wording than several private schools that ban everything beyond spelling and grammar (Brown, S1).
